Why health and safety feels hidden, and how to fix it

I walked every H&S page live on the .com as admin, manager, team lead and support worker. The pages work and the rules are right. They feel hidden and AI-made because one accident is split across two records that disagree, the next step rarely comes with its button, and everything is said in the system's voice with the same weight.

The ten things that hurt most

P1every role

One accident, two records that disagree

The incident says "Waiting for an owner · Safety review: done". Its safety review says "Actions under way, stage 3 of 5" and "Awaiting independent verification". Two owners, two progress lines, two close lists.

P1admin · manager · team lead

The next step has no button

"Choose a new owner", but the only button is "Start action". "No safety review owner yet", with no way to assign. A manager reads "You can read this review" and nothing more.

P1admin · manager · team lead

Buttons you can't use, work that isn't yours

A team lead's Close button works until they submit, then the server refuses anyone but the owner. Managers get "Accept" rows that open read-only. Some reviews can't close at all without "close anyway".

P1admin · team lead

The everyday steps are hidden

Start, mark done and check an action live only in ⋮ and right-click menus. Eleven pages print "right-click a row for options".

P1admin · manager · team lead

Ownership is the bottleneck

Most safety reviews and incidents say "Needs an owner". Only accepting an incident handover sets an owner, so chemical exposure, restraint and drill reviews can never get one, and no owner can be changed later.

P1admin · manager

The waiting state cries wolf

While the Overview loads, "Needs attention could not be loaded" shows for a few seconds although nothing failed (the H&S session is fixing this now). The headline numbers arrive last; board M2 designs the wait.

P1admin · H&S officer · team lead

A failed fix can close a review

Checking an action as "it didn't work" still counts as done, so the safety review can close with no replacement action. Found in audit round 2; decision Q11.

P2every role

It talks like the system

A disclaimer on every page, close checks like "Linked Control Room alert has the same Site scope", "Fire_evacuation drill", five words for "waiting to be checked".

P2admin · team lead

Hubs are directories

"Injuries and care" opens four plain cards with no numbers. The lists behind them already have the right tab rail, so it's an extra click that tells you nothing.

P2support worker · manager

The start doesn't fit the role

Support workers find reporting at the bottom right of My Day, next to a second report button. Managers get a support worker's start and a queue they can't act on.

Why the workflows feel weird

Built from the tables, not the jobIncident, review, investigation, action and WorkSafe each got their own page. "Deal with this fall" spans four pages that don't agree.
Permissions enforced, not designedPages show the whole feature and let the server say no. They never say who can do a step.
Privacy as disclaimers"Records you can access", "Restricted", "Not available" as text everywhere instead of quiet, consistent states.
Legal completeness over guidanceCopy explains the system instead of saying what to do next.
No hierarchyOne card style for urgent work, zero counts and help text alike.
Approved fixes half-built"One accident, one place" and "hubs as tabs" were approved in Part 2; only their smaller parts were built.
Each page works out status aloneNobody checks one record across every page, so titles and statuses drift.

Why Parts 1–4 didn't fix the feel

They fixed symptoms page by page inside inherited layouts. The "user test" checked that things worked and were allowed, not what a person does next. Most time went on permissions and speed. The two big structural drawings were approved but only partly built. The demo data still reads like test data. This round started from each role's next step instead, checked one record across every page, put every fix with a check, and had the plan and boards audited eight times by reviewers with no earlier context, until a round found nothing major.
