# New Zealand supported-living governance: researched decisions
Audit date: 11 September 2026. Requested by the owner during this audit. These are product defaults informed by guidance, not a claim that every supported-living provider has the same constitution or legal structure.

## What the authoritative sources establish
- A charity's governing document determines how its governing body works. The application must record the relevant constitution/trust deed and committee terms, rather than treating its hard-coded thresholds as authority. [Charities Services governance guidance](https://www.charities.govt.nz/im-a-registered-charity/running-your-charity/governance-for-charities).
- Incorporated-society constitutions must address committee quorum/voting and separately general-meeting procedures. A society's general-member written resolution is a different mechanism from a board decision: section 89 provides a 75% minimum of entitled members where the constitution permits it. Do not import that percentage into board/committee voting. [Companies Office constitution guidance](https://www.is-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz/help-centre/running-your-incorporated-society/your-societys-constitution/), [Incorporated Societies Act, section 89](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2022/0012/37.0/LMS100900.html). The full latest Act fetch was blocked; the cited section was available in official search extraction.
- Trusts have a statutory default duty of unanimity, subject to the Act and trust terms; therefore simple majority cannot be asserted as a universal charitable-trust rule. [Trusts Act 2019, duties](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2019/38/en/latest/sections/DLM7382824/).
- IoD describes majority quorum as common, subject to a constitution. It recommends early, digestible papers stating whether an item seeks information, discussion or a decision, including financial implications; a week for pre-reading is guidance, not a statutory deadline. Its annual work-plan examples include accounts, strategy, risk, CEO performance and committee terms. [IoD: How a board works](https://www.iod.org.nz/resources-and-insights/starting-a-board/how-a-board-works).
- Charities Services recommends disclosure, recorded management of conflicts, and in many cases withdrawal from discussion, voting and quorum. It also recognises justified alternatives; the organisation's rules/policy must govern any exception. The software must distinguish recusal from abstention. [Charities Services conflicts guidance](https://www.charities.govt.nz/im-a-registered-charity/running-your-charity/conflict-of-interestpanga-rongorua).
- Ngā Paerewa NZS 8134:2021 remains the published standard in the Ministry's current guidance. Applicability is modular and depends on service/certification/contract; “supported living” alone does not establish that every criterion applies. [Ministry of Health standard overview](https://www.health.govt.nz/regulation-legislation/certification-of-health-care-services/services-standard).
- Sector guidance section 2.1 supports board induction, governance/management separation, current terms of reference, service-user/lived-experience input, planned quality/risk reporting and equity information. It supports a board experience that explains service outcomes and accountable action; it does not prescribe app voting percentages or an access-role matrix. [Ngā Paerewa sector guidance, governance](https://www.health.govt.nz/publications/sector-guidance-for-nga-paerewa-health-and-disability-services-standard-nzs-81342021).
- Personal information needs reasonable safeguards against unauthorised use/disclosure; job-based access is supported by the Privacy Commissioner's guidance. Health/disability information requires the applicable Health Information Privacy Code controls (the official page lists an amended version in force 1 May 2026). Use de-identified board assurance wherever sufficient. [Privacy Principle 5](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/5/), [OPC employee access guidance](https://www.privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/knowledge-base/view/90/), [current HIPC](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/codes-of-practice/hipc2020/).
- Independent remuneration-committee oversight is a recognised governance approach; it does not mean all raw appraisal material must be sent to every director. The source is corporate guidance, used here as an analogy, not a supported-living legal mandate. [IoD NZ Code of Practice](https://www.iod.org.nz/assets/Documents/Policies/IoD-Code-of-Practice-for-Directors-2022.pdf).

## D1 — Voting and quorum product decision
Implement a small, versioned rule profile per board/committee using existing membership and meeting records. Do not create a general legal rules engine.
Proposed ordinary board-meeting default: quorum floor(N/2)+1 of current entitled voting seats; N is the entitled roster before item-specific recusals. Recused members do not contribute to present/participating count; do not shrink the required quorum automatically. Zero eligible voters never makes quorum. Ordinary decision passes if for > against among valid expressed votes, once quorum is met; abstentions remain separate. Ties fail; no casting vote by default.
Committee electorate is its current appointed voting membership and documented delegation, not all board members or everyone with a matching committee-type string. Observer, adviser, executive contribution and secretary administration confer no vote by themselves. An appointed voting secretary/treasurer remains a voting board member; the job title alone cannot decide eligibility.
Written board resolutions are disabled until the governing authority permits them. Proposed enabled default is assent from all entitled voters; absence, no response and abstention are not assent. A recusal under that profile blocks completion and directs the secretary to the approved alternate process; the implementer must not lower the denominator. “Unanimous” explicitly means every entitled voter, not merely everyone who cast for/against. Special thresholds, casting votes, proxies and lawful conflict exceptions must be explicitly approved/configured; existing two-thirds records remain historical, not silently reinterpreted.
Freeze paper version, electorate IDs, rule version, attendance/recusal evidence and tally at opening/closure as specified in GOV-W03/W04. Revocation prevents future actions immediately but does not rewrite completed history. A material electorate/rule/paper change during voting requires cancellation and reissue, with a link to the superseded decision.
Release gate D1: record actual legal form, governing-document/version and approved rule values before live voting/approval is enabled. The user has authorised researching defaults, not supplied a constitution. All engineering and synthetic testing can proceed using the documented candidate profile.

## D2 — Executive and CEO review privacy product decision
Use record audiences with capability checks, not a blanket board-wide or admin-wide entitlement.
- Ordinary published board business: current authorised board members/approved observers; executives only for their assigned contribution/publication audience. Drafts belong to authors and assigned reviewers.
- Full-board in-committee discussion: explicitly recorded non-conflicted board audience, excluding CEO/executives unless invited for a defined item. It need not be chair-only. Committee sessions use the assigned committee audience.
- Restricted personnel, complaints, legal and individual safeguarding papers: chair or designated non-conflicted alternate plus assigned committee/reviewers and necessary minute-taker; invite others explicitly with recorded purpose. Technical administration is not routine access.
- CEO appraisal working material/raw feedback: assigned chair and performance/remuneration reviewers. CEO can enter their own self-assessment and read shared goals/final communicated assessment; no automatic access to other respondents' identifiable feedback or board deliberations. Full board gets an approved decision/summary appropriate to its responsibilities. Grant fuller access through a recorded governance/legal process when needed; the application is not a mechanism for denying lawful information rights.
Apply the audience to query, count, search, calendar, activity, attachments, PDF, notification and direct-object paths. A parent audience limits children. Attendance/RSVP is not an invitation mechanism. Preserve evidence and privacy-safe audit history when access changes.
This is a researched least-privilege product default, not a universal legal rule. GOV-W02 and W18 can implement it now. Release configuration needs the actual non-conflicted chair/alternate and committee appointments; unknown existing restricted records require chair review, with no bulk board-wide backfill.

## D3 — Supported-living information scope
Keep service-user outcomes, quality/safety, rights/complaints, workforce capability, financial sustainability, risks, compliance and strategic delivery visible as assurance summaries. Reuse current clinical, Te Tiriti, Finance and Roadmap sources. Show source coverage/period and accountable owner. Do not add individual clinical records or fabricated compliance obligations merely to fill dashboard panels. A service lead must confirm which Ngā Paerewa/contract obligations apply; log this as an external content-validation gate for GOV-W16/W21, not an excuse to delay truthful empty/unconfigured states.
